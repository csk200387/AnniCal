National Paper Airplane Day Hero
Jack Northrop
Jack Northrop (1895-1981) was an American aircraft industrialist and designer, known for founding Northrop Corporation. A pioneer in aviation, he built his first glider in 1916 and later developed innovative flying wing designs. Northrop's early experiments with flight, including using paper models to test aerodynamic principles, laid foundational groundwork for modern aircraft, making him a fitting figure to represent the spirit of paper airplane innovation.

National Paper Airplane Day FAQs 
When is National Paper Airplane Day? 
In 2027, National Paper Airplane Day falls on Wednesday, May 26. It’s a perfect mid-week opportunity to take a break from routine and engage in the simple, creative fun of aerogami.
How many paper airplanes are folded annually? 
The practice of folding paper airplanes continues to be a widespread activity, with countless designs shared and tested. Enthusiasts from school children to engineers contribute to the vast number of planes crafted, making it one of the most common forms of amateur aviation.
What are the basic types of paper airplanes? 
Beyond basic gliders and darts, there are also stunt planes capable of loops and turns, and even complex models resembling real aircraft. Each type requires different folding techniques and paper weights to achieve its intended flight characteristics.
What is the world record for paper airplane flight? 
The pursuit of paper airplane records continues to inspire innovation in design and throwing technique. These impressive feats demonstrate the potential for simple paper to achieve remarkable aerodynamic performance, pushing the boundaries of what’s possible with a folded sheet.
National Paper Airplane Day Activities
Design your own paper airplane
This is the easiest and most fun way to participate in National Paper Airplane Day. Channel your inner child and get crafty! You can use watercolors, markers, or glitter. What’s your favorite color? Your paper airplane represents you, fly high!
Organize a paper airplane contest
Grab some co-workers or friends and head outside. Utilize your social media to find interested contestants. Most contests offer two basic flight categories: distance and time in air, but you can add more categories to your contest. Don’t forget the prizes.
Teach a kid to make a paper airplane
You may not believe it, but paper airplane are truly becoming a lost pastime. Celebrate paper airplanes and National Paper Airplane Day by teaching America’s youth the art and joy of making a paper airplane. It might be hard at first to pull your kids or nieces and nephews away from their computers, but once they do, you’ll all have a blast making planes together and flying them in the sunshine. 
Why We Love National Paper Airplane Day
They let us get artistic
There aren’t many excuses in this modern life to let your inner artist out, but making a paper airplane is one of them! Folding and decorating a paper airplane is the perfect opportunity to bring out your arts and craft kit. Get crafty! Personalize your paper plane with paint, markers, crayons, glitter — the sky is the limit.
It doesn’t take technology to participate
We could all use a few minutes away from our smart phones and computers. Building a paper airplane allows you to unplug from technology, relax, get outside, and get some fresh air. (But if you must bring your phone, it makes a great Snapchat story.)
It’s a great way to relive your childhood
Time flies. Remember those sunny Saturday afternoons you spent with your best friend in the park? Sometime life can be hectic, but building a paper airplane can take you back to those days you flew paper airplanes with your BFF and had ice cream afterward.
